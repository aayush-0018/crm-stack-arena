# Growth Ideas — More Customers, More Repeat Visits, Smarter Stock

30 ideas for helping a local shop owner grow, built on the data the CRM already collects: customers with phone numbers, every order with its date, time, items, amounts and payment status.

Each idea notes **how it would work in the app** and **why it helps the shop**. Ideas marked ⚠ need an outside service (WhatsApp Business API, SMS gateway, Google); the rest work with local data alone.

---

## A. Getting new customers (6)

| # | Idea | How it works in the app | Why it helps |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Referral codes** | Every customer gets a short code (e.g. `RAMESH50`). When a new customer gives that code at billing, the new customer gets a first-order discount and the referrer gets credit on their next bill. The app tracks who referred whom. | Word of mouth is how local shops grow. This makes it measurable and rewards the people who already bring friends. |
| 2 | **Shareable digital catalogue** | A simple page/PDF listing products, prices and the shop's WhatsApp number, generated from the product list. The owner shares it in society and building WhatsApp groups. | People in nearby buildings discover the shop and order without visiting first. |
| 3 | **Home-delivery zones** | Record each customer's area/society; the app shows which areas customers come from and which nearby areas have few. Add free-delivery thresholds by area. | Shows where to put up flyers or offer delivery to win the next neighbourhood. |
| 4 | ⚠ **Google Business Profile helper** | After a good order, the invoice/WhatsApp message carries a "Rate us on Google" link. The dashboard tracks how many review requests went out. | Most first-time customers find local shops through Google Maps; more reviews mean more walk-ins. |
| 5 | **First-visit welcome offer** | The app recognises a new phone number at billing and suggests a small welcome coupon valid on the second visit within 14 days. | The hardest visit is the second one; this gives a reason to come back. |
| 6 | **Bulk-order / event leads** | A "wedding, puja or event order" form that captures date, headcount and an estimated list, with follow-up reminders before the date. | Event orders are large and come through word of mouth; tracking them stops leads being forgotten. |

## B. Repeat customers & retention (7)

| # | Idea | How it works in the app | Why it helps |
|---|---|---|---|
| 7 | **"Running out soon" reminders** | For items bought regularly (atta, oil, rice), learn each customer's usual gap (e.g. 10 kg atta every ~25 days). A few days before it's due, list them on a "Due for refill" screen with one-tap WhatsApp: *"Your atta may be running low — shall we keep 10 kg ready?"* | Turns habit into reliable repeat orders, and catches the customer before they buy elsewhere. |
| 8 | **Lapsed-customer win-back** | Flag customers whose gap since their last order is much longer than their normal gap (e.g. a weekly buyer silent for 3 weeks). Show a "Customers slipping away" list with their usual basket and a personal message. | Getting back a lost regular is far cheaper than finding a new one. |
| 9 | **Customer segments (RFM)** | Score customers on Recency, Frequency and Money spent, and group them: *Champions, Regulars, New, At risk, Lost*. Each group gets a suggested action. | The owner sees at a glance who to thank, who to nudge and who to win back, instead of treating everyone the same. |
| 10 | **Simple loyalty stamps** | "Every 10th order ₹50 off" or points per ₹100, shown on every invoice ("3 more visits to your reward"). | Visible progress keeps customers coming back to the same shop instead of the one next door. |
| 11 | **One-tap "Same as last time"** | On the customer page and in WhatsApp replies, re-create the last order at current prices for the owner to confirm. | Removes friction for repeat buyers; phone/WhatsApp orders take seconds. |
| 12 | **Monthly ration subscription** | A customer sets a standing monthly list (grains, oil, dal). The app creates the order on the chosen date and reminds the owner to pack and deliver. | Locks in the customer's biggest monthly spend; gives the shop predictable demand. |
| 13 | **Repeat-rate dashboard** | Track the % of customers who come back within 30/60 days, the average days between visits, and new vs returning revenue, month by month. | Shows whether retention efforts are working, with numbers rather than guesses. |

## C. Idle hours vs peak hours (6)

| # | Idea | How it works in the app | Why it helps |
|---|---|---|---|
| 14 | **Traffic heatmap** | A weekday × hour grid from `orders.created_at` showing orders and revenue per slot; quiet slots are highlighted automatically. | Shows the shop's real rhythm, which is often different from what the owner assumes. |
| 15 | **Idle-hour happy offers** | The owner sets an offer for quiet slots (e.g. *2–5 pm weekdays: 5% off snacks*). The app applies it automatically at billing during those hours and reports the uplift. | Fills dead hours with sales instead of discounting during rush hours when customers come anyway. |
| 16 | **Pre-order for pickup in quiet slots** | Customers WhatsApp their list; the shop packs it during idle hours and the customer picks it up during the evening rush without waiting. | Moves packing work into idle time and shortens rush-hour queues, so fewer customers walk away. |
| 17 | **Staff & task planner** | Using the heatmap, suggest when an extra helper is needed (peaks) and when to do restocking, cleaning and deliveries (idle). | Better service at peaks, lower staff cost, and nothing gets missed. |
| 18 | ⚠ **Targeted idle-hour broadcasts** | Send an offer only to customers who usually shop at those hours or live close by, *before* the quiet slot (e.g. a 1 pm message for a 2–5 pm offer). | Relevant, well-timed offers work better and annoy fewer people than blasting everyone. |
| 19 | **Rush-hour express billing** | During detected peak hours, the billing screen switches to a fast mode: favourites grid, last-ordered items and keyboard shortcuts. | Faster checkout at peak hours = more customers served, fewer lost to long queues. |

## D. Seasonal vs non-seasonal demand (6)

| # | Idea | How it works in the app | Why it helps |
|---|---|---|---|
| 20 | **Festival calendar** | A built-in Indian calendar (Diwali, Holi, Navratri, Eid, Raksha Bandhan, Pongal/Onam, Chhath, wedding season, school reopening, monsoon), editable by region. The dashboard counts down to the next one. | Festivals drive a large share of a year's sales for a local shop; preparation starts weeks before. |
| 21 | **Seasonal item tagging** | Learn from past sales which items spike around each festival or season (ghee and dry fruits before Diwali, colours before Holi, umbrellas in monsoon) and which sell steadily all year. | Separates "stock up now" items from everyday staples, so money isn't tied up in the wrong stock. |
| 22 | **Last year's same-period report** | "Last Diwali week you sold 40 kg ghee and 25 boxes of sweets; top buyers were …" shown 3–4 weeks before the festival. | Concrete numbers for ordering and for knowing which customers to call first. |
| 23 | **Festival hampers & combo builder** | Suggest combos from items often bought together in that season (e.g. a puja kit or a Diwali gift box), priced from cost plus margin. | Raises average order value and gives the shop something special to promote. |
| 24 | **Off-season demand boosters** | In slow months, find items with steady demand and suggest bundles or "buy more, save more" offers (e.g. a monthly ration pack). | Smooths out cash flow between festivals. |
| 25 | **Seasonal pre-booking** | Customers reserve festival items (sweets, gift boxes, puja items) in advance with an optional advance payment; the app turns bookings into a purchase list. | Removes guesswork from festival stocking and secures the sale before competitors do. |

## E. Inventory management (5)

| # | Idea | How it works in the app | Why it helps |
|---|---|---|---|
| 26 | **Smart reorder suggestions** | For each product: average daily sales, days of stock left, and supplier lead time, giving a suggested order quantity. Seasonal spikes (#21) raise it ahead of festivals. | Avoids both running out (lost sales) and over-ordering (blocked cash, expiry). |
| 27 | **Dead & slow stock finder** | Lists items with no sales in 30/60/90 days and the money tied up in them, with a one-tap "clearance bundle" suggestion. | Frees up cash and shelf space for things that actually sell. |
| 28 | **Expiry & batch tracking** | Record the expiry date on purchase; alert 15/30 days before and suggest discounting near-expiry items to customers who buy them regularly. | Cuts waste, which matters most for dairy, packaged food and medicines. |
| 29 | **ABC analysis** | Rank items by revenue: **A** (the top ~20% bringing most revenue), **B** and **C**. Never let A items run out; review C items before reordering. | Puts the owner's attention and cash where they matter most. |
| 30 | **Supplier price & margin tracker** | Record supplier purchase prices over time, compare suppliers per item, and show margin per item and the margin lost when prices rise. | Better buying and pricing decisions; spots when a selling price needs updating. |

**Note on test data:** the seeded test orders use made-up order times (between about 10 am and 8 pm), so the hour-based ideas (#14–19) only show meaningful patterns once real orders build up.

## Compliance before any messaging

- **Consent:** India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 expects customers to agree before their phone number is used for promotions. Store an opt-in flag and the date it was given, and honour opt-outs.
- **Bulk SMS** needs DLT registration under TRAI rules. **WhatsApp broadcasts** need the WhatsApp Business Platform with approved message templates.
- **One-to-one click-to-chat** messages sent from the owner's own WhatsApp (`wa.me` links) avoid these platform requirements, but should still respect opt-outs.

## Suggested order to build

1. **#14 Traffic heatmap** and **#13 Repeat-rate dashboard**: they use existing data, take only a day or two each, and show the owner where they stand.
2. **#9 Segments** → **#8 Win-back list** → **#7 Refill reminders** (using click-to-chat): the biggest repeat-revenue lever, with no outside service needed.
3. **#20 Festival calendar** + **#22 Last-year report**: ready before the next big festival.
4. **Product catalogue** (from FEATURE_IDEAS.md #9), then **#26 Reorder suggestions**, **#27 Dead stock** and **#29 ABC**: the inventory ideas depend on it.
5. **#15 Idle-hour offers** and **#1 Referrals**: once there are a few weeks of real traffic data to tune them.
